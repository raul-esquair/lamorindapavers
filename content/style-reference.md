# Voice Reference — Lamorinda Pavers

> This file is read by the AI drafting pipeline as a "voice anchor." Both the drafting pass and SEO critique pass load it verbatim into every prompt. If you want to shift the voice of future posts, edit here.

## Tone

Warm, design-forward, polished. We're writing to East Bay homeowners with budget for craft, who care how their property looks from the street and during entertaining. Practical when it counts (real numbers, real timelines, real warranty terms) but never clinical. The reader is doing research — we respect that and don't waste their time.

Second person throughout. "Your driveway," "your property," "your lifestyle." Never "our clients" or "homeowners often..."

## Sentence rhythm

Mix short and medium. Avoid long compound sentences. Vary opening structures — don't start three paragraphs in a row with the same construction.

Contractions are fine. Use them.

Lead with specifics. Instead of "Many factors influence cost," say "Five things move the number: lot size, slope, paver pattern, base prep depth, and access." Bullet lists are fine when the content is genuinely a list — don't break up real prose just to add visual variety.

## What we never do

- Generic contractor language a competitor could write verbatim ("we pride ourselves on quality," "industry-leading craftsmanship," "world-class customer service")
- Filler adjectives stacked together ("premier, top-tier, unmatched")
- Vague hedge phrases ("typically," "generally," "in many cases" — when we have data, name it)
- Talking down to the reader. Assume they've done their homework.
- Sentences over 30 words unless absolutely necessary

## What we always do

- Name specific places and constraints: Happy Valley Road in Lafayette, the Orinda hills, downtown Walnut Creek corridor, Moraga's clay slopes, Danville's flatter lots near the freeway
- Cite real numbers: install timelines in days, paver costs per sq ft, warranty terms, resealing cadence, base prep depth in inches
- Use the actual vocabulary of paving: herringbone, running bond, edge restraints, joint sand, base aggregate, ICPI standards, interlocking concrete pavers, modular blends
- Address the reader as "you"
- Frame outcomes around lifestyle: Saturday entertaining, curb appeal that holds up across seasons, year-round outdoor usability

**Example 3 — FAQ answer (paver driveway durability):**

Interlocking pavers are 3-4 times stronger than poured concrete and flex with ground movement instead of cracking. Individual pavers can also be replaced if ever damaged — you don't have to redo the whole driveway because a delivery truck nicked one corner. On an expansive-clay lot in Lafayette or Orinda, that flex matters: poured slabs hairline-crack within a few seasons. Pavers don't.

---

**Example 4 — local context (Lafayette driveway):**

Lafayette homeowners along Happy Valley Road and up into the hillside neighborhoods tend to face two specific issues we plan for: drainage on sloped lots, and clay-soil base preparation. A standard 4-inch base aggregate depth fails inside two years on most Lafayette grades — we build to 8 inches with a compacted decomposed-granite layer underneath. It costs more up front. It also doesn't fail.
