## What is AEO, and how does it help your business?

**SEO (Search Engine Optimization)** is what most contractor websites optimize for: ranking blue links high enough that prospects click them.

**AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)** is different. AEO is about making your content the source AI engines quote when they synthesize an answer for a user.

Concrete example. A homeowner in Walnut Creek asks ChatGPT: *"Do paver patios add value to a home?"* The AI's answer reads something like: *"Paver patios increase home value 60–75% of project cost in East Bay luxury markets, recovering $20K–$45K on a typical $30K–$60K install. They outperform concrete patios by 15–25 percentage points of ROI because of better clay-soil resilience..."* Followed by citations.

The goal is to be the cited source for that answer. Every article in this 23-post library is engineered to deliver the kind of self-contained, factually-precise, locally-grounded content that AI engines pull from.

The mechanical levers we use on every article:

- **Direct-answer sentences** at the top of every section (the AI engines quote these verbatim)
- **Specific numbers and ranges** rather than vague generalities ("$25,000–$75,000," not "varies widely")
- **Named industry standards** (ICPI Tech Spec 2, ASTM C33, CSLB license codes, NFPA 58 propane code)
- **Named local entities** (Lafayette, Orinda Downs HOA, Happy Valley Road, the Orinda hills)
- **Named brand SKUs** (Belgard Holland Stone, Calstone Country Manor, Techniseal HP NextGel)
- **Entity definitions** as quotable standalone sentences ("Expansive clay is soil that swells when wet and shrinks when dry, with seasonal volume change of 5–10%.")
- **Stat callouts** rendered as standalone sentences AI Overviews quote directly
- **Brand co-occurrence** with cities and services in factual context — this is how AI engines learn to associate "Lamorinda Pavers" with "best paver installer in Lafayette" queries

All 23 articles incorporate these techniques. The publishing engine has been configured to apply them by default, so every future post — including the auto-generated batches that follow this initial library — carries the same AEO posture.

## Expected impact

This is a real investment of time and attention. Setting honest expectations.

### What this strategy is designed to deliver

**Visibility in AI search results.** Within 3–6 months of consistent publishing, expect to see Lamorinda Pavers cited in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews for East Bay paver queries. AI engines recrawl content on a 30–60 day cycle; citation generally starts surfacing after the third or fourth article in a cluster is indexed.

**Traffic from long-tail queries.** The articles target hundreds of distinct query phrasings — not the five most competitive keywords contractors usually fight over. Expect 30–100 organic visits per month per article, scaling as the library grows. Year-one traffic projection: 8,000–15,000 organic sessions across the library.

**Higher-intent leads.** The decision-stage articles (vetting, budgeting, scope, kitchen-specific cost overruns) attract homeowners actively researching their project — not browsing. Expect lead-quality lift on form submissions from organic traffic compared to your pre-strategy baseline.

**Authority signal that compounds.** Each article cross-links to four to five others in the library, building a topical authority graph that search engines and AI engines both reward. By month six, the library functions as a coordinated resource that AI engines can navigate to answer almost any East Bay paver question.

**Resale-aware buyer attraction.** Three articles specifically address resale value impact. This attracts homeowners 1–3 years from listing — the highest-value moment of decision making for premium upgrades.

### What this strategy is not designed to deliver

- **Same-week lead spikes.** Content marketing compounds; it doesn't trigger flash demand. Expect early traffic in months 1–2 but meaningful lead volume from month 3 onward.
- **Replacement for paid advertising.** This is a long-term moat, not a short-term campaign. Both can run in parallel.
- **One-shot results.** The first article publishes May 18; the last publishes October 19. The library only fully functions as an authority-building system once most of it is live.

### Realistic timeline

**Month 1 (May).** First two articles live. Indexed by Google within 2–3 weeks. Early AI citation possible on Lafayette-specific queries.

**Months 2–3 (June–July).** Cluster pieces start cross-linking. Authority signal builds. Long-tail traffic begins. The first commercial-intent decision-stage articles publish, likely generating the first qualified inquiries from organic search.

**Months 4–5 (August–September).** Resale and driveway clusters publish. Lead volume should be measurably higher than baseline. AI citation should be regular for at least 5–10 of the target queries.

**Month 6+ (October onward).** Library fully built. AI citation should be regular across most target queries. Other paver-related content may begin citing these articles as references, generating organic backlinks.

## How the content is produced

This isn't a manual writing pipeline. The articles are drafted and revised by an editorial engine that:

1. Reads each detailed brief (all 23 are written and locked in already)
2. Drafts the article using your brand voice, anchored to a style reference document we built specifically for Lamorinda Pavers
3. Runs an SEO and AEO critique pass that adds FAQ sections, internal links from a site inventory, stat callouts, entity definitions, and brand-city co-occurrence
4. Generates a featured image
5. Opens a draft pull request on your behalf for review

You'll receive an email every Friday morning with the draft for the following Monday's post. The review pattern is "leave the email open by Sunday night = approve, reply with notes = revise, close = reject." Most weeks, the draft is publishable as-is with light edits.

After this initial 23-article library publishes, the system auto-proposes the next batch when the queue runs low — keeping the publishing cadence weekly indefinitely without re-planning sessions.
